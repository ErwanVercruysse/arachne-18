How do we measure longer strides?

Reference tests measure both body travel per gait cycle and the actual body-relative foot excursion, averaged over all six feet and complete cycles. Cadence, slipping, body tilt and motor-target smoothness are checked separately. All recordings play at simulation time. The 26 checks cover directions and perturbations at a 0.24 m/s command, plus forward walking at 0.34 m/s. The maximum-speed gait needs the tested flat surface: separate friction changes at that speed exceed the existing limits. Read the measured comparison and validation bounds.

Maximum height and simulation sensitivity

The highest standard-model jump reaches 2.51 cm collision clearance with a 1 ms physics step, versus 1.94 and 1.84 cm with finer steps. A separate robust policy reaches 2.08, 2.04 and 2.01 cm across those same settings. Both retain the original joint and servo limits. The objective rewards the lowest point of the entire collision model, so lifting the body while leaving a foot grounded earns no jump reward. These are the best policies found in this search; landing recovery and hardware jumping have not been validated.

What do these films demonstrate?

Each film is a continuous 12-second reference MuJoCo/BAM simulation at real-time playback, using the general policy and seed 701. All five recorded runs pass their respective gates. Stone contacts affect support and collision checks; flexible grass contacts are measured separately. Three forward layouts each pass for small stones and the new dense short grass, but the full small-stone admission test reaches 29/30 and 28/30 passes, below the required threshold in its second batch. Larger stones and mixed obstacles still need training. These examples do not establish performance in every direction or in real grass. The previous heightfield admission result remains unchanged; its 8 mm plateau stage is still unmastered.

Smoothness is part of the reward.

Training rewards tracking the requested velocity and turn rate. Tilt, bouncing, slipping and abrupt motor accelerations cost points. Learned gait parameters build on a rhythmic pattern with alternating support legs.

CEM searches directly for better controller parameters. The IMU supplies tilt and angular velocity; the controller does not use perfect position or linear velocity measurements from the simulator.

Fast training. Independent checks.

The general controller was trained first on forward motion, then on multiple directions and turning. Checks use the original MuJoCo model and full BAM servo model, including intermediate directions, added weight and IMU noise.

This is a digital prototype. The 12 V servo parameters are approximations; performance on real hardware has not been validated.
